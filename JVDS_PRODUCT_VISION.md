# JVDS Product Vision

## Read This Before Working On JVDS

JVDesignStudio exists so young people can learn, create and play without barriers.

It grew from two connected roots:

1. A family of characters and stories that made creativity feel warm, personal and possible.
2. The real-world problem of teaching young people on restricted school computers where downloads, installs, admin rights, accounts, paid software and powerful hardware often are not available.

JVDS is a browser-first creative studio. The browser is not a compromise. It is the access layer that lets learners start making things in the environment they actually have.

Creativity shouldn't need permission.

## Product North Star

A new learner should be able to arrive at JVDS and make something they are proud of within 15 minutes.

Progress is not measured by how many pages, tools or feature cards ship. Progress is measured by what learners are able to create, understand, play, save, share and improve.

## The Core Loop

Every meaningful JVDS experience should support this loop:

Imagine -> Learn -> Create -> Play -> Improve -> Imagine again

The characters are part of the product identity and should guide the learner's emotional journey:

- Stardust: Imagine. Sparks possibility, wonder and the next idea.
- Lumo: Learn. Makes concepts feel clear, kind and approachable.
- Ember: Create. Helps learners build, experiment and bring ideas into the world.
- Pip: Play. Encourages testing, curiosity, joy and discovery.
- Echo: Improve. Helps learners reflect, iterate and make the next version better.

The characters are not decoration. They are the heart of JVDS and should help learners know how to feel and what to do next.

## The School-Computer Rule

Core JVDS experiences should require:

- No installation
- No admin rights
- No paid software
- No powerful PC
- No account required to start

Open the browser. Start creating.

This rule is why the toolbox matters. Browser tools, generators, editors, workshops, games, downloads and lightweight creative utilities are solutions to real classroom and school-device restrictions. Do not remove or dismiss the toolbox as clutter. Improve it, connect it and make it feel like one creative system.

## Before You Build Anything

Before adding, changing or prioritising work, ask:

- Does this help a young person make, understand, play or improve something?
- Does it strengthen the core creative journey?
- Can it work in a restricted school/browser environment?
- Can an existing tool be improved instead of creating another disconnected one?
- Does it respect the characters as part of the identity and heart of JVDS, rather than decoration?
- Is this genuinely more important than improving the learner's first successful creation?

If the answer is no, do not build it yet.

## Agent-Facing Guidance

Future agents should read this document before making architectural, UX, content or feature decisions.

The dev board backlog is not a to-do list. It is a map of possibilities, evidence, obligations and ideas. Prioritise against the North Star, the school-computer rule and the current mission.

Prefer work that:

- Makes the first successful creation easier.
- Connects existing tools into the Imagine -> Learn -> Create -> Play -> Improve loop.
- Improves clarity, saving, exporting, onboarding, accessibility and mobile/browser reliability.
- Preserves low-friction access for school devices.
- Uses characters to support meaning, mood and guidance.

Be careful with work that:

- Adds another standalone page without connecting it to the learner journey.
- Requires installs, signups, admin rights or paid software for the core experience.
- Treats the characters as mascot stickers instead of product identity.
- Optimises internal page count instead of learner outcomes.
- Makes the first 15 minutes harder.

## Current Mission

Make the first successful creation feel obvious, joyful and achievable on a locked-down school computer.

A learner should be able to:

1. Arrive and understand where to begin.
2. Pick a clear first creative path.
3. Make something small but real.
4. Save, export or keep evidence of the work.
5. Know one thing they can improve next.

Everything else should orbit that mission until it is reliably true.
