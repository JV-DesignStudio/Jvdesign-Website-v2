Scratch Studio

Build a Scratch game right here, play it, then take it into Scratch for real. Pick a game, set up the sprites and values, and when you are happy, download a real Scratch project file (.sb3) you can open on scratch.mit.edu or in the offline editor.

Companion to the My First Scratch Game series. Nothing to install here, it works on a school computer.

🦊
Lumo says: go at your own pace. Press Play, change one value, and press Play again. When it feels fun, export it and open it in Scratch to keep going.
Make your own art and sounds: draw sprites in Pixel Studio and make music or sound effects in Sound Studio, then bring them into your game.

Sprite

A character or object, like the cat or an apple. Each one has its own blocks.

Variable

A box that holds a value, like Score. The studio adds one for you.

Stage

The background and the code that runs for the whole project.

Your blocks

These are the Scratch blocks the studio builds for you. Each colour is a different block type, just like in Scratch. Read them, or rebuild them by hand in Scratch.

Play your game

This is a preview of the game the blocks make. Export it to open the real thing in Scratch.

Take it into Scratch

This downloads a real Scratch project file (.sb3) with your sprites, costumes and blocks already in place.

How to open it in Scratch

  1. Go to scratch.mit.edu and sign in (or open the offline Scratch editor).
  2. Click Create, then File menu, then Load from your computer.
  3. Pick the .sb3 file you downloaded. Your game opens with all the sprites and blocks.
  4. Press the green flag and play. Change anything you like, it is yours now.

No Scratch account yet? You can still read the blocks guide and build it yourself in Scratch for free.
