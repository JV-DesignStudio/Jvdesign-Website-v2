Build the rally in six steps

Lumo says: do not build everything at once. Get the car driving, then make the camera feel good, then add the clock. Each step ends with something you can play.

Project setup tip: use metres in Blender and in Godot. A rally car is about 1.8 m wide and 4 m long. If your first import looks like a toy, check the scale before you touch the physics.
1

Block out a stage worth driving

A start gate, three sectors and a finish. Rough is good at this stage.

Rally is not a race track. It is a narrow road across the ground, with blind crests, a surface that changes under the wheels, and corners you have to read before you reach them. Keep the first stage short: 60 to 90 seconds is plenty.

  • Width: about 7 m. Wide enough for mistakes, narrow enough to feel tight.
  • Surface: tarmac for the first section, gravel in the middle, maybe mud near the finish.
  • Corners: one fast sweeper, one hairpin and one blind crest. Do not build twenty corners on day one.
  • Markers: put a visible gate at the start, a board at each sector and a big finish banner.

Fast way to start: block the road out in the browser with the 3D World Builder, export the OBJ, then import it into Godot. Nothing to install and you can see the shape before you commit.

Godot way: add a CSGBox3D or a GridMap for the road, and a large StaticBody3D with a ConcavePolygonShape3D for the ground. Give the gravel sections a group name so the car can find them later.

2

Model a low-poly rally car in Blender

One body, four wheels, under about 1,500 triangles.

Low-poly does not mean lazy. It means every shape has a job. Build the body from a cube, then add a cabin, wheel arches, lights and a small spoiler. Keep the wheels as separate objects so Godot can spin and steer them.

  1. Delete the default cube and add a new cube. Scale it to about 1.8 x 0.9 x 4.0 (X, Y, Z in Blender).
  2. Add a smaller cube for the cabin. Move it up and slightly back, then join it to the body.
  3. Make one wheel from a cylinder with 12 sides. Rotate it so it rolls around the X axis. Duplicate it four times.
  4. Name the body CarBody and the wheels Wheel_FL, Wheel_FR, Wheel_RL and Wheel_RR.
  5. Put the origin of every wheel at the centre of that wheel. Put the car origin at the middle of the chassis, at ground level.
  6. Apply rotation and scale to everything with Ctrl+A.
Which way is forward? Point the nose of the car along Blender -Y. The glTF exporter turns that into Godot +Z, which is VehicleBody3D's forward. If the car drives backwards later, rotate the model 180 degrees before you touch the code.

Add a little colour with materials. Flat colours suit low-poly. Give the body a bright rally colour, the glass a dark blue, and the wheels a dark grey.

Save the Blender file as rally-car.blend next to the export. You will come back to it when you want a second car or a livery.

Import the car and make it drive

A VehicleBody3D with four wheels that moves, steers and stops.

Create a new Godot 4 project called LowPolyRally. Copy rally-car.glb into the project folder and let Godot import it.

Build this scene tree. The names matter, because the script looks them up.

RallyStage (Node3D)              # main scene
├── WorldEnvironment
├── DirectionalLight3D
├── Stage (imported mesh or CSG)
├── Car (VehicleBody3D)          # script: car.gd
│   ├── CollisionShape3D         # BoxShape3D, about 1.8 x 1.0 x 4.0
│   ├── CarBody (MeshInstance3D) # the body mesh from the .glb
│   ├── Wheel_FL (VehicleWheel3D)
│   ├── Wheel_FR (VehicleWheel3D)
│   ├── Wheel_RL (VehicleWheel3D)
│   └── Wheel_RR (VehicleWheel3D)
├── CameraRig (Node3D)           # script: camera_rig.gd
│   └── SpringArm3D
│       └── ShakePivot (Node3D)
│           └── Camera3D
├── HUD (CanvasLayer)
└── StageTimer (Node)            # script: stage_timer.gd

For each wheel, put a MeshInstance3D of that wheel inside the VehicleWheel3D. Set use_as_steering on the two front wheels and use_as_traction on the two rear wheels.

Start with these wheel values, then adjust them in step 4:

SettingValueWhy
Wheel radius0.35 mMatches a chunky rally tyre.
Suspension travel0.25 mEnough bump for gravel, not a boat.
Suspension stiffness35Firm enough to corner.
Damping compression2.5Stops the car bouncing after a crest.
Damping relaxation3.5Lets the wheel drop back down.

extends VehicleBody3D
class_name RallyCar
# car.gd

@export var engine_power := 50.0
@export var brake_power := 25.0
@export var reverse_power := 35.0
@export var handbrake_power := 25.0
@export var max_steer := 0.5
@export var steer_speed := 3.5
@export var top_speed_kmh := 140.0
@export var camera_rig: Node3D

@onready var start_transform := global_transform
@onready var ground_ray: RayCast3D = $GroundRay
@onready var rear_wheels: Array[VehicleWheel3D] = [$Wheel_RL, $Wheel_RR]

var steer := 0.0
var surface_grip := 1.0

func _ready() -> void:
    contact_monitor = true
    max_contacts_reported = 4
    body_entered.connect(_on_body_entered)

func _physics_process(delta: float) -> void:
    _read_surface()

    var throttle := Input.get_action_strength("accelerate")
    var brake_input := Input.get_action_strength("brake")
    var handbrake := Input.is_action_pressed("handbrake")
    # Positive steering turns left, so ask for left as the positive action.
    var steer_input := Input.get_axis("steer_right", "steer_left")

    # Slowly turn the wheels instead of snapping them.
    steer = move_toward(steer, steer_input * max_steer, steer_speed * delta)
    steering = steer

    var speed := linear_velocity.length()
    if brake_input > 0.01:
        engine_force = 0.0
        brake = brake_input * brake_power
        # At a standstill, the brake pedal becomes reverse.
        if speed < 0.6:
            engine_force = -reverse_power * brake_input
    elif speed * 3.6 < top_speed_kmh:
        # A little extra push at low speed so the car gets moving.
        var boost := 1.0
        if speed < 5.0 and speed > 0.1:
            boost = clampf(5.0 / speed, 1.0, 2.0)
        engine_force = throttle * engine_power * boost * surface_grip
        brake = 0.0
    else:
        engine_force = 0.0
        brake = 0.0

    if handbrake:
        brake = handbrake_power
        for wheel in rear_wheels:
            wheel.wheel_friction_slip = 1.4

    if Input.is_action_just_pressed("reset"):
        reset_to_start()

func _read_surface() -> void:
    if not ground_ray.is_colliding():
        surface_grip = 0.6
        return

    var collider := ground_ray.get_collider() as Node
    if collider and collider.is_in_group("gravel"):
        surface_grip = 0.75
    elif collider and collider.is_in_group("mud"):
        surface_grip = 0.55
    else:
        surface_grip = 1.0

func _on_body_entered(_body: Node) -> void:
    var impact := linear_velocity.length()
    if impact > 4.0 and camera_rig:
        camera_rig.add_trauma(clampf(impact / 18.0, 0.15, 1.0))

func reset_to_start() -> void:
    global_transform = start_transform
    linear_velocity = Vector3.ZERO
    angular_velocity = Vector3.ZERO

Add a RayCast3D as a child of the car, pointing down, with target_position about Vector3(0, -1.2, 0). Drag the CameraRig into the car's camera_rig slot in the Inspector.

If the car drives the wrong way: flip the steering sign first. If it still fights you, the model is facing the wrong way. Fix the model, not the physics.
4

Tune the rally feel

Grip that changes with the surface, a handbrake that rotates the car, and a reset that saves the run.

A rally car should feel like it is moving on top of the road, not glued to it. Gravel has less grip than tarmac. Mud has less again. The handbrake should let the rear step out, not stop the car dead.

Grip per surface

SurfaceGrip multiplierFriction slipHow it should feel
Tarmac1.03.0Sharp, planted, quick to change direction.
Gravel0.752.2Loose at the rear, longer slides.
Mud0.551.8Heavy, slow to turn, easy to bog down.

Keep a copy of your best handling numbers in a note. Small changes are easier to judge than big ones. Change one value, drive one corner, then change the next.

# Add this to car.gd if you want per-wheel grip.
# It runs after _read_surface(), so surface_grip is already set.

@onready var wheels: Array[VehicleWheel3D] = [
    $Wheel_FL, $Wheel_FR, $Wheel_RL, $Wheel_RR
]

func _apply_grip() -> void:
    var slip := 3.0 * surface_grip
    for wheel in wheels:
        wheel.wheel_friction_slip = slip

# Then call _apply_grip() in the else branch of the handbrake check in
# _physics_process(), so the handbrake keeps its low rear grip.
Handbrake feel: the handbrake slows the rear wheels and drops rear grip. In code, set a lower friction slip on the rear wheels while the handbrake is held, and a higher brake value. Release it and the full grip comes back.

Restart from A

A sprint is one clean run from A to B. If you crash, press R and start again. The sprint timer resets the car to its start transform, puts it back on the line and runs the countdown again. No checkpoints and no respawns halfway.

Why no checkpoints? A checkpoint reset would let you skip the part you crashed on, which is not a sprint time. If you want a longer rally later, add service points that reset the car but add a penalty to the clock.
Pip's test: drive badly on purpose, roll the car, then press R. The car should be back at A, still, with the countdown running. If it keeps moving, the frozen flag is not being set.
5

Build the DiRT-style camera rig

Three views, a spring that keeps the camera out of hills, corner look-ahead, speed FOV and impact shake.

The camera is the reason a rally game feels fast. In games like DiRT Rally, the camera is not welded to the car. It sits in the world, lags behind the car, leans into corners and shakes when you hit something.

The three views

Chase

Behind and above

The default. The camera swings behind the car, pulls back a little at speed and looks toward where the car is going, not where it has been.

Bonnet

On the hood

Fast, low and wide. This view is rigid, so every bump is felt. It is the view that makes a narrow road feel narrow.

Cockpit

Inside the car

Closest and most intense. Sit slightly off-centre, lower the FOV and let the shake be strongest here. Best for replays and short runs.

What makes it feel like DiRT

  • The camera lives in world space, not as a child of the car.
  • A spring arm stops the camera from going through hills and banks.
  • It looks ahead into the corner, using the steering input and the car's velocity.
  • Speed pulls the camera back and widens the FOV, but only a little.
  • Impacts add trauma. Trauma decays, so the shake settles by itself.
  • Each view has its own FOV and its own shake strength.
  • Smoothing uses delta time, so it feels the same on a slow laptop and a fast PC.

Build the rig

Start with the scene tree from step 3. Add a SphereShape3D to the SpringArm3D with a radius of about 0.3. Add the CameraRig to a group called camera_rig only if you prefer groups. In this guide the car holds a direct reference, so no group is needed.

Attach this script to CameraRig, then drag the SpringArm3D, ShakePivot and Camera3D into the matching slots.

extends Node3D
# camera_rig.gd

enum View { CHASE, BONNET, COCKPIT }

@export var car: RallyCar
@export var spring_arm: SpringArm3D
@export var shake_pivot: Node3D
@export var camera: Camera3D

@export_group("Chase")
@export var chase_distance := 6.5
@export var chase_height := 2.1
@export var chase_look_height := 1.0
@export var chase_smooth := 6.0
@export var corner_look := 1.8

@export_group("Fixed views")
@export var bonnet_offset := Vector3(0.0, 1.05, 1.4)
@export var cockpit_offset := Vector3(0.35, 1.15, -0.2)

@export_group("Feel")
@export var base_fov := 62.0
@export var max_fov := 82.0
@export var bonnet_fov := 80.0
@export var cockpit_fov := 72.0
@export var fov_smooth := 5.0
@export var trauma_decay := 1.4
@export var shake_strength := 0.09

var view := View.CHASE
var trauma := 0.0
var _noise := FastNoiseLite.new()

func _ready() -> void:
    _noise.frequency = 1.8
    _noise.seed = randi()
    spring_arm.add_excluded_object(car.get_rid())
    _set_view(View.CHASE)

func _unhandled_input(event: InputEvent) -> void:
    if event.is_action_pressed("camera_next"):
        _set_view((view + 1) % 3)
    elif event.is_action_pressed("camera_prev"):
        _set_view((view + 2) % 3)

func _set_view(next_view: int) -> void:
    view = next_view
    if view == View.CHASE:
        spring_arm.collision_mask = 1
        spring_arm.spring_length = chase_distance
    else:
        spring_arm.collision_mask = 0
        spring_arm.spring_length = 0.0

func _physics_process(delta: float) -> void:
    if car == null:
        return

    if view == View.CHASE:
        _update_chase(delta)
    elif view == View.BONNET:
        _update_fixed(bonnet_offset)
    else:
        _update_fixed(cockpit_offset)

    _update_fov(delta)
    _update_shake(delta)

func _update_chase(delta: float) -> void:
    var speed := car.linear_velocity.length()
    # VehicleBody3D forward is +Z, so the car basis Z axis is the way ahead.
    var forward := car.global_transform.basis.z
    var flat_forward := Vector3(forward.x, 0.0, forward.z).normalized()
    # Positive steering is left, and +X is the model's left side.
    var steer := Input.get_axis("steer_right", "steer_left")

    var pivot := car.global_position + Vector3.UP * chase_height
    var look_target := pivot + flat_forward * (1.0 + speed * 0.03)
    look_target += car.global_transform.basis.x * steer * corner_look

    var desired := Transform3D().looking_at(look_target - pivot, Vector3.UP)
    desired.origin = pivot

    # The spring arm casts along its local +Z, so the camera ends up behind.
    global_transform = global_transform.interpolate_with(
        desired, 1.0 - exp(-chase_smooth * delta)
    )

func _update_fixed(offset: Vector3) -> void:
    # The camera looks down its local -Z. Turn the rig around so that -Z
    # points the same way as the car's +Z forward.
    var desired := car.global_transform
    desired.basis = desired.basis.rotated(Vector3.UP, PI)
    desired.origin += car.global_transform.basis * offset
    global_transform = desired

func _update_fov(delta: float) -> void:
    var speed_ratio := clampf(car.linear_velocity.length() / 40.0, 0.0, 1.0)
    var target := lerpf(base_fov, max_fov, speed_ratio)

    if view == View.BONNET:
        target = bonnet_fov + speed_ratio * 8.0
    elif view == View.COCKPIT:
        target = cockpit_fov + speed_ratio * 5.0

    camera.fov = lerpf(camera.fov, target, 1.0 - exp(-fov_smooth * delta))

func add_trauma(amount: float) -> void:
    trauma = clampf(trauma + amount, 0.0, 1.0)

func _update_shake(delta: float) -> void:
    trauma = maxf(trauma - trauma_decay * delta, 0.0)
    var shake := trauma * trauma
    var t := Time.get_ticks_msec() * 0.001

    shake_pivot.position = Vector3(
        _noise.get_noise_2d(t * 18.0, 0.0),
        _noise.get_noise_2d(0.0, t * 18.0),
        0.0
    ) * shake_strength * shake

    shake_pivot.rotation = Vector3(
        _noise.get_noise_2d(t * 13.0, 5.0),
        _noise.get_noise_2d(5.0, t * 13.0),
        _noise.get_noise_2d(t * 9.0, 9.0)
    ) * deg_to_rad(3.0) * shake
Why the spring arm? It casts a shape backward from the pivot. If a bank or tree is in the way, the camera slides in closer instead of showing the inside of a hill. Excluding the car's own body stops it from colliding with the car.
If the chase camera faces the wrong way: the model is probably facing the wrong way. VehicleBody3D's forward is +Z (Vector3.MODEL_FRONT). Swap looking_at(look_target - pivot) for the opposite direction only after you have checked the car model.
Pip's camera test: drive the same corner in all three views. Chase should swing smoothly. Bonnet should feel planted and busy. Cockpit should be the most intense. If all three feel the same, the fixed views are not actually rigid.
6

Run it as an A-to-B sprint

A countdown at A, a clock, a finish at B and a best time to beat.

A rally sprint is a single run from A to B. The car waits at A, a short countdown runs, then the clock starts. The run ends when the car crosses the finish at B. No laps and no respawns. If you crash, press R and run it again from A.

Add an Area3D at A for the start sign, three along the route for splits and one at B for the finish. The start sign shows the line. The countdown is what starts the clock.

extends Node
# stage_timer.gd - the A-to-B sprint

signal countdown_updated(value: float)
signal race_started
signal split_taken(number: int, seconds: float)
signal stage_finished(total: float, splits: Array, best: float, is_new_best: bool)
signal time_updated(seconds: float)

enum State { COUNTDOWN, RUNNING, FINISHED }

@export var countdown_seconds := 3.0
@export var car: RallyCar
@export var finish_gate: Area3D
@export var sectors: Array[Area3D] = []

const BEST_PATH := "user://low_poly_rally_best.cfg"

var state := State.COUNTDOWN
var countdown_left := 3.0
var start_time := 0.0
var splits: Array[float] = []
var next_sector := 0
var best_time := 0.0

func _ready() -> void:
    if finish_gate != null:
        finish_gate.body_entered.connect(_on_finish)
    for i in sectors.size():
        sectors[i].body_entered.connect(_on_sector.bind(i))
    load_best()
    restart_race()

func _process(delta: float) -> void:
    if Input.is_action_just_pressed("reset"):
        restart_race()
        return

    if state == State.COUNTDOWN:
        countdown_left -= delta
        countdown_updated.emit(countdown_left)
        if countdown_left <= 0.0:
            state = State.RUNNING
            start_time = _now()
            car.frozen = false
            race_started.emit()
    elif state == State.RUNNING:
        time_updated.emit(_now() - start_time)

func restart_race() -> void:
    state = State.COUNTDOWN
    countdown_left = countdown_seconds
    splits.clear()
    next_sector = 0
    car.reset_to_start()
    car.frozen = true
    countdown_updated.emit(countdown_left)
    time_updated.emit(0.0)

func _on_sector(body: Node3D, index: int) -> void:
    if body != car or state != State.RUNNING or index != next_sector:
        return
    splits.append(_now() - start_time)
    next_sector += 1
    split_taken.emit(index + 1, splits[-1])

func _on_finish(body: Node3D) -> void:
    if body != car or state != State.RUNNING:
        return
    state = State.FINISHED
    car.frozen = true
    var total := _now() - start_time
    var is_new_best := best_time <= 0.0 or total < best_time
    if is_new_best:
        best_time = total
        save_best(total)
    stage_finished.emit(total, splits, best_time, is_new_best)

func load_best() -> void:
    var cfg := ConfigFile.new()
    if cfg.load(BEST_PATH) == OK:
        best_time = float(cfg.get_value("sprint", "best_time", 0.0))

func save_best(total: float) -> void:
    var cfg := ConfigFile.new()
    cfg.set_value("sprint", "best_time", total)
    cfg.save(BEST_PATH)

func _now() -> float:
    return Time.get_ticks_msec() / 1000.0

Show the clock and the best time

Keep the HUD in the corners. The countdown can sit in the middle, but only for a few seconds. The best time is read from the same file, so it survives a restart.

extends CanvasLayer
# hud.gd

@onready var time_label: Label = $TimeLabel
@onready var split_label: Label = $SplitLabel
@onready var result_label: Label = $ResultLabel
@onready var countdown_label: Label = $CountdownLabel
@onready var stage_timer: Node = get_node_or_null("../StageTimer")

func _ready() -> void:
    stage_timer.connect("countdown_updated", _on_countdown)
    stage_timer.connect("race_started", _on_race_started)
    stage_timer.connect("split_taken", _on_split)
    stage_timer.connect("stage_finished", _on_finish)
    stage_timer.connect("time_updated", _on_time)
    _show_best(float(stage_timer.get("best_time")))

func _on_countdown(value: float) -> void:
    countdown_label.visible = true
    countdown_label.text = str(ceili(value)) if value > 0.0 else "GO!"

func _on_race_started() -> void:
    countdown_label.text = "GO!"
    await get_tree().create_timer(0.8).timeout
    countdown_label.visible = false
    result_label.visible = false

func _on_time(seconds: float) -> void:
    time_label.text = "TIME  %0.2f" % seconds

func _on_split(number: int, seconds: float) -> void:
    split_label.text = "SPLIT %d  %0.2f" % [number, seconds]

func _on_finish(total: float, splits: Array, best: float, is_new_best: bool) -> void:
    time_label.text = "FINISH  %0.2f" % total
    result_label.visible = true
    result_label.text = "NEW BEST!" if is_new_best else "BEST  %0.2f" % best
    var parts := PackedStringArray()
    for s in splits:
        parts.append("%0.2f" % s)
    split_label.text = "Splits: " + ", ".join(parts)

    result_label.visible = true
    result_label.text = "BEST  %0.2f" % best if best > 0.0 else "BEST  --"

Add pacenotes, not a sat nav

A co-driver calls the corner before you see it. You do not need voice acting for the first version. A small text callout above the road is enough. Give each corner a severity from 1 to 6, where 1 is a hairpin and 6 is flat out.

CallMeaningWhat the driver does
Right 6Very fast rightLift slightly, stay in the racing line.
Left 4Medium leftBrake before the corner, then power out.
Hairpin leftVery tight leftSlow hard, use the handbrake if needed.
CrestBlind topStay straight, do not turn on the crest.
Simple build: put an Area3D 40 m before each corner. On body_entered, show the note for three seconds. After that, hide it. That is enough to change how the stage feels.
Echo's finish check: start a run, cross the finish at B, then press R. The countdown should restart, the car should be back at A, and the best time should still be there. If the clock starts before GO, or the best time resets on restart, the sprint state machine is wrong.

Echo's recovery check

Stuck? Try this

These are the three things that most often stop a first 3D rally game. Check the symptom first, then try the fix.

The car falls through the stage or sinks into it

What you'll see: the car drops out of the world, or the wheels disappear under the road.

Fix: check that the stage has a StaticBody3D with a collision shape, and that the car's collision box is above the wheels, not inside them. If the whole world looks tiny, your Blender export scale is wrong. Re-export with Apply Modifiers and check the scale in Godot.

The car spins on gravel and will not straighten up

What you'll see: every small steering input turns into a spin, and the rear steps out on a straight.

Fix: raise the rear wheel friction slip, reduce the steering angle, or lower the engine force. Also check that the rear wheels are the only traction wheels. If all four wheels drive and steer, the car becomes nervous.

The camera clips into hills or jitters

What you'll see: the camera jumps inside a bank, or shakes even when the car is still.

Fix: give the SpringArm3D a SphereShape3D, exclude the car from its collision, and make sure the smoothing uses delta time. If the camera jitters when parked, the shake trauma is not returning to zero, or the spring arm is colliding with the car.

Still stuck? Drop the car and camera into an empty scene with one flat plane. If it drives there, the problem is the stage. If it does not, the problem is the car setup.

Finish in Godot

Make it a game you can keep

  1. Download the free low-poly-rally-starter.zip if you want the finished scene layout.
  2. Press F5 and drive the full stage. Check the start, every split and the finish.
  3. Test all three cameras with C. Make sure the chase camera does not clip on the tightest bend.
  4. Open Project > Export, add a Windows Desktop or Web preset, then choose Export Project.
  5. Keep the whole export folder, not just the .exe, so the game still runs when you copy it.
  6. Take one screenshot of a jump and one of the cockpit view for your dev log.
