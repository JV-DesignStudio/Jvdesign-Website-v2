A World Is a Grid of Tiles
The simple idea underneath a whole world
Active
🎯
Goal for this step

Picture a world as a grid where every square is one tile.

  • 1A Terraria world is a huge grid. Every square in the grid is one tile, like grass, dirt, stone or water.
  • 2Wherever you dig, you are changing tiles from one kind into another. Digging makes empty space.
  • 3Because it is a grid, an editor like TEdit can show you the whole world at once and let you paint tiles.
  • 4That is why "editing a world" and "painting tiles" are the same thing.

Foreground Tiles vs Walls
Two layers behind every room
Locked
🎯
Goal for this step

Know the difference between the tile you stand on and the wall behind it.

  • 1The foreground is the tile you can stand on or bump into: stone, dirt, wood, platforms.
  • 2The wall sits behind it. It does not block you, but it makes a space count as a room.
  • 3A wall is what stops enemies spawning in a safe space. No wall, and a room is not really a room.
  • 4In TEdit you switch between placing tiles and placing walls, just like the Wall mode button in the Studio.

Chests, Signs and Metadata
The extra lists a world keeps
Locked
🎯
Goal for this step

Understand that a world also stores objects and small pieces of data.

  • 1A chest is an object placed at a tile. It has a list of items inside it.
  • 2A sign stores a line of text you can read in the game.
  • 3Other small pieces of data, like which NPCs have moved in, are called metadata.
  • 4All of this lives in the same one .wld file as the tiles. One world, one file.

The World Header
Name, size and version at the top
Locked
🎯
Goal for this step

Know what the header of a world file tells the game.

  • 1Every world starts with a header. It is a small block of facts about the world.
  • 2The header holds the world name, its size and the game version that made it.
  • 3If the version in the header is newer than your game, the world will not load. That is the most common red line.
  • 4It also stores the world's seed, the little code that helped shape it.
A world header
name:     MyFirstWorld
size:     medium
version:  1.4.x

Backups Keep You Safe
A copy means nothing is ever lost
Locked
🎯
Goal for this step

Make copying a world a habit, not an afterthought.

  • 1Terraria already keeps a .bak copy next to each world. That is a safety net.
  • 2Before you edit, make your own copy with a clear name, like TestWorld.
  • 3Do all your experiments on the copy. If it breaks, you just delete it and copy again.
  • 4Backups are the single most useful habit in this whole series.

Locked

Goal for this step

Open the Worlds folder and name what you see.

  • 1Open the Worlds folder using the paths on the cheat sheet.
  • 2You should see one .wld per world, and a matching .wld.bak for most.
  • 3If a world has a little picture next to it in the game menu, that picture is a separate small file.
  • 4Do not rename files here by hand. Let the game and TEdit handle names, so nothing gets lost.
