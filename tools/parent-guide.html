What is JVDesignStudio?

JVDesignStudio is a free, browser-based platform where children and young people learn to make their own games. There are over 170 interactive workshops covering game engines like Scratch, Godot, Unity, Unreal and Roblox, plus 32 games and creative tools.

Everything runs directly in the web browser — there is nothing to install, no account required, and no in-app purchases. It was built specifically for young game developers, with age-appropriate content and a safe, ad-free environment.

Key Facts

  • 160 workshops across 5 game engines and coding languages
  • 40+ browser games to play and learn from
  • Creative tools for pixel art, sound design, map building and more
  • 100% free — no accounts, no ads, no paywalls
  • Works on any device with a modern web browser

How to Get Started

Getting started is simple. Here is a step-by-step guide for the first visit:

  1. Visit the website — Go to jvdesignstudio.co.uk in any modern web browser (Chrome, Firefox, Safari, Edge all work).
  2. Choose an age group — The homepage has three pathways: Little Steps (ages 5-7), The Lab (ages 8-12), and Teen Dev Hub (ages 13+).
  3. Pick a workshop — Each age group has a curated list of workshops sorted by difficulty. Start with the easiest ones.
  4. Follow along — Workshops are step-by-step interactive guides. Each step has clear instructions and the child follows along in the game engine.
  5. Save progress — Progress is saved locally in the browser. Use the Progress page to track completion.

No Setup Required

There is no software to install. The workshops guide children through downloading the right tools (like Scratch or Godot) when needed, but many workshops run entirely in the browser.

Choosing the Right Workshop

Not every workshop is suitable for every age. Here is a guide to help you pick the right starting point:

Age Group Recommended Engine Difficulty Notes
5-7 years Scratch Easy Visual block-based coding. Great first step.
8-10 years Scratch, GDevelop Easy Can follow step-by-step with minimal help.
10-12 years GDevelop, Godot, Roblox Medium Ready for more complex logic and real scripting.
12-14 years Godot, Unity, GameMaker Medium Can work independently on most workshops.
14+ years Unity, Unreal, Godot, Python Advanced Full coding workshops and professional tools.

Tip for Younger Children

For children under 8, we recommend sitting together for the first few sessions. Scratch workshops are visual and intuitive, but reading the step-by-step instructions is easier with a grown-up nearby.

Supervising Your Child

You do not need to be a programmer to support your child. Here is when to help and when to let them explore:

When to Step In

  • First session — Help them navigate to the right workshop and show them how the step-by-step interface works.
  • Installing software — Some workshops (Godot, Unity) require downloading the engine. Help with this part.
  • Frustration — If they are stuck, encourage them to re-read the current step. The instructions are designed to be followed carefully.
  • Saving work — Remind them to save regularly. The site tracks progress, but the actual game files are on their computer.

When to Step Back

  • Exploring — Let them click around and experiment. Making mistakes is part of learning.
  • Playtesting — When they run their game, watch them play and ask what they think.
  • Creative choices — Let them decide what their game looks like, what characters to use, what the story is.
  • Problem-solving — If they ask "why doesn't this work?", ask them "what do you think might be wrong?" before jumping in.

The Best Support

The most helpful thing you can do is show genuine interest. Ask them to show you what they have built. Ask questions like "How did you make that work?" or "What are you going to add next?" — this keeps them motivated and thinking critically.

Screen Time Guidelines

Screen time is a common concern. Here is a balanced approach for game development learning:

Recommended Session Lengths

  • Ages 5-7: 20-30 minutes per session, with a break
  • Ages 8-12: 30-45 minutes per session
  • Ages 13+: 45-60 minutes per session, with breaks

Making Screen Time Productive

  • Active vs passive — Game development is active creation, not passive consumption. It engages problem-solving, creativity and logic.
  • Set clear start/end times — Use a timer or agree on a number of workshop steps to complete.
  • Encourage breaks — Follow the 20-20-20 rule: every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds.
  • Balance with other activities — Pair screen time with outdoor play, reading, or other hobbies.
  • Joint sessions — Sometimes sit with them. Ask them to teach you what they have learned. This turns screen time into quality time.

Signs It Might Be Time for a Break

Rubbing eyes, slouching, losing focus, or becoming irritable are all signs it is time to step away from the screen. The workshops save progress, so they can always come back later.

Troubleshooting

Common Issues

The page looks broken or blank

  • Try a different browser (Chrome is recommended)
  • Clear the browser cache: Settings > Privacy > Clear browsing data
  • Make sure JavaScript is enabled (it is by default in all modern browsers)

Progress is not saving

  • Progress is saved in the browser's local storage. If they use a different browser or clear their data, progress may be lost.
  • Encourage them to complete workshops in one sitting when possible, or use the same browser each time.

A workshop requires software download

  • This is normal for Godot, Unity, Roblox and similar engines. Help your child download from the official website (links are provided in the workshop).
  • All downloads are from official sources — never from third-party sites.

The game engine is slow or crashing

  • Close other browser tabs and applications
  • Restart the browser
  • Check that the computer meets the engine's minimum requirements

Sound is not playing

  • Click anywhere on the page first — browsers block autoplay audio
  • Check the system volume and browser tab is not muted

Privacy & Safety

JVDesignStudio is designed with children's safety as a top priority:

No Accounts Required

Children do not need to create an account, provide an email, or share any personal information to use the site. There are no sign-up forms, no login pages, and no social features.

What Data Is Collected

  • Anonymous analytics — We use privacy-focused analytics (GA4) that tracks page views and usage patterns. No personal data is collected.
  • No cookies for tracking — We do not use advertising cookies or third-party tracking cookies.
  • Local storage only — Game progress and preferences are stored locally in the browser. This data never leaves the device.

COPPA Compliance

JVDesignStudio is fully compliant with COPPA (Children's Online Privacy Protection Act) and GDPR requirements for children's data:

  • No personal information is collected from any user, including children
  • No accounts or registration is required
  • No external links to social media or advertising
  • All content is age-appropriate and manually curated
  • External links (to game engines) go only to official, verified websites

External Links

Some workshops link to external websites (like the Godot or Unity download pages). These links open in a new tab and go only to official, verified sources. We never link to user-generated content platforms or social media.
