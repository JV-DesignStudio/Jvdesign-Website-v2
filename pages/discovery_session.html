The Origin Story

Born for my children. Grown for everyone.

Every character started as a real feeling I watched my own children carry - something they couldn't name yet. I made each one so they'd have something to hold onto. Now they help anyone who needs them.

🦊
Lumo - the Anxious Fox
Created when I watched my child spiral over things nobody else could see. Lumo names the invisible weight of anxiety - the "grumble-grit" - and shows that carrying something hard doesn't mean you're broken. Lumo helps children understand their own feelings aren't wrong, just heavy.
🐢
Pip - the Brave Turtle
Born from bedtime. The lights-off panic, the midnight door creaks, the ceiling shadows. Pip turns the dark from something frightening into something full of wonder. Pip helps little ones face the dark - and find out it was never the enemy.
🦎
Echo - the Resilient Axolotl
Made for the moments when a child gives up at the first hard step. Echo doesn't leap mountains - they take one small honest step, then another. Echo teaches that courage isn't a sudden burst. It's the choice to try again, quietly.
🐯
Ember - the Creative Tiger
Ember came from watching a child decide they "weren't creative" before they'd ever been given the right tools. Ember is bold, messy and unstoppable. Ember is the voice that says: pick up the tool, make the thing, worry about perfect later.
✨
Stardust - the Cosmic Pup
For every child who ever felt like they didn't quite belong - in their class, their family, their skin. Stardust travels the universe looking for where they fit, and finds out the answer was never about fitting. Stardust helps children feel that belonging starts from the inside out.
🔮
Elara - the Wise Owl
Created because children go online before they're ready, and nobody gives them a map. Elara holds the Wire-Web Key - the tools to stay safe, stay kind and stay themselves in digital spaces. Elara helps children think before they click, and feel confident in a connected world.

"I didn't set out to build a character universe. I set out to help two small people feel less alone - and somewhere along the way, it became something for everyone."
